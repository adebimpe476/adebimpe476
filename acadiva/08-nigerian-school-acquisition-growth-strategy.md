# 🎯 ACADIVA — Nigerian School Acquisition & Growth Strategy

<b>Version:</b> 1.1<br /><b>Status:</b> Operating strategy / hypotheses to validate<br /><b>Market scope:</b> Nigerian schools only<br /><b>Initial launch market:</b> Ibadan, Oyo State

## 1. Executive direction

ACADIVA will acquire schools in Nigeria only during the current go-to-market phase. International schools and international customer acquisition are out of scope until the Nigerian model is proven.

The immediate objective is not to collect a large list of schools or registrations. It is to secure a small number of Nigerian schools that agree to test ACADIVA in real operations, use the relevant workflows independently, provide structured feedback and make a credible paid-continuation decision.

<b>Positioning:</b> ACADIVA is digital operating infrastructure for Nigerian schools — bringing school administration, student records, academic workflows, fee visibility and parent communication into one connected system.

Start with newly established private schools and schools preparing to open. They may be more willing to set up digital processes before paper-based habits become entrenched. Validate this assumption through direct conversations rather than treating it as proven.

## 2. Geographic focus

### Phase 1 — Ibadan

Begin in Ibadan, Oyo State. Concentrate visits and outreach in a manageable set of communities rather than spreading effort across the whole city.

Initial target: recruit 3–5 credible founding schools in Ibadan. This is a target, not a claim that schools have already agreed.

### Phase 2 — Southwest expansion

After the first pilots produce evidence, expand to:

- Lagos State
- Ogun State
- Osun State
- Other Southwest locations where qualified school leads and support capacity exist

### Phase 3 — Wider Nigeria

Expand into other Nigerian states only when onboarding, product reliability, support and acquisition economics can be repeated without relying entirely on founder-led intervention.

<b>No international acquisition campaign is planned in this strategy.</b>

## 3. Priority school segments

| Priority | School segment | Why approach them | What to validate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Very high | Schools under construction or preparing to open | Opportunity to establish digital processes before opening | Opening date, approvals, decision-maker, budget and readiness |
| Very high | Newly established private schools | May still be setting up records, admissions and parent communication | Current tools, pain points and willingness to adopt |
| High | Existing private schools using paper, spreadsheets or disconnected tools | Clear potential administrative pain | Cost of current process, staff readiness and migration effort |
| Medium | Established private schools already using another platform | Possible switch or integration opportunity | Dissatisfaction, switching cost and unmet needs |
| Low initially | Public schools and large institutional systems | Potentially longer procurement and approval processes | Procurement path, authority and implementation requirements |

Target nursery, primary, junior secondary and senior secondary schools, including schools combining multiple levels. Do not assume every school needs every ACADIVA module.

## 4. School discovery channels

Use a mix of online research and physical discovery. Record where each lead came from so the team can learn which channels produce qualified conversations.

### A. Online discovery

- Search engines and map listings for private schools, new schools and schools announcing new campuses.
- Instagram, Facebook and other public social pages for school launch announcements, construction updates, admission campaigns and opening dates.
- School websites and public admission notices.
- Job advertisements for new schools recruiting principals, teachers, administrators, bursars or registrars. Hiring activity can signal that a school is preparing to open or expand.
- Public notices from relevant Nigerian education authorities where accessible and appropriate.

### B. Physical discovery

- Visit communities with visible school construction or new school signage.
- Speak with school suppliers, printers, uniform vendors, bookshops, furniture vendors and educational equipment providers who may know schools preparing to open.
- Visit school clusters and introduce ACADIVA to proprietors or administrators by appointment.
- Ask existing school contacts for introductions to other proprietors.

### C. Associations and ecosystem partners

Explore relationships with:

- Private-school associations, including relevant state and local chapters.
- Education consultants and school setup advisers.
- Teacher and school leadership networks.
- School suppliers and service providers.
- Local technology and implementation partners.

Treat introductions as a route to conversations, not as proof of endorsement. Do not imply an association or authority endorses ACADIVA without written permission.

## 5. Lead qualification

A school should not be treated as a qualified opportunity merely because its name or phone number has been found.

### Minimum qualification questions

1. Is the school operating, preparing to open or under construction?
2. Where is it located — state, LGA and community?
3. What school levels does it serve or plan to serve?
4. Who is the proprietor, founder or authorised decision-maker?
5. When does it expect to open, if it is not yet operating?
6. How does it currently manage student records, attendance, results, fees and parent communication?
7. Which of these processes causes the most difficulty?
8. Does the school have the staff and willingness to participate in onboarding and a pilot?
9. Who approves software purchases and what is the likely procurement process?
10. What would the school need to see before paying for ACADIVA?

### Lead scoring proposal

Score each factor from 0 to 2:

- Decision-maker access
- Urgency of the problem
- Readiness to adopt
- Fit with ACADIVA's current product capabilities
- Ability and willingness to pay

Use the score to prioritise follow-up, not to automatically reject schools. Record the evidence behind each score.

## 7. Acquisition pipeline

Use consistent stages:

1. <b>Discovered</b> — school identified from a source.
2. <b>Verification pending</b> — basic existence, location or status needs checking.
3. <b>Verified</b> — school and key details checked against reliable evidence or direct contact.
4. <b>Decision-maker identified</b> — proprietor or authorised buyer identified.
5. <b>Qualified</b> — need, product fit and readiness discussed.
6. <b>Outreach sent</b> — first contact made and recorded.
7. <b>Follow-up</b> — waiting for response or next conversation.
8. <b>Demo scheduled</b> — a specific demonstration has been agreed.
9. <b>Pilot discussion</b> — scope, responsibilities, duration and success criteria being agreed.
10. <b>Pilot onboarding</b> — school is configuring and using ACADIVA.
11. <b>Active pilot</b> — agreed workflows are being used and measured.
12. <b>Converted</b> — school has agreed to a paid subscription.
13. <b>Deferred / lost</b> — reason recorded; follow-up only where appropriate.

Do not count a registration, demo or verbal expression of interest as a converted customer.

## 8. Founding Schools Programme

Recruit an initial cohort of 3–5 Nigerian schools, beginning in Ibadan.

### What ACADIVA may offer

Subject to product readiness and a written pilot agreement:

- Assisted school setup and configuration.
- School profile and digital presence where available.
- Administrator and teacher account setup.
- Student and admission record setup.
- Relevant academic and attendance workflows.
- Parent access and communication features that are ready.
- Training for the staff participating in the pilot.
- A defined support channel and feedback sessions.
- Preferential introductory pricing or a time-limited pilot arrangement.

Do not promise features that are not built, tested or ready for that school.

### What ACADIVA should request in return

- A named school decision-maker and pilot coordinator.
- Staff participation in onboarding and training.
- Use of agreed workflows during the pilot.
- Structured feedback and permission to investigate reported issues.
- Permission before using the school's name, logo, testimonials or results in public marketing.
- A clear decision meeting at the end of the pilot, including whether the school will pay to continue.

Avoid unlimited free access or lifetime-free arrangements. Any subsidised pilot should have a defined duration, scope and conversion discussion.

## 9. Demonstration and sales process

A demo should begin with the school's actual workflow, not a generic tour of every screen.

### Suggested demo sequence

1. Ask the proprietor or administrator how the school currently handles a typical school task.
2. Identify the most costly or frustrating part of that process.
3. Demonstrate only the relevant ACADIVA workflow.
4. Show how the proprietor, administrator, teacher and parent experiences differ where those features are ready.
5. Explain data access, role permissions, school separation and support honestly.
6. Identify what is live, what is in pilot and what remains on the roadmap.
7. Agree on a next step: a second meeting, pilot assessment or a clear no.

### Pilot readiness checklist

- [ ] Decision-maker has approved participation.
- [ ] School has nominated a coordinator.
- [ ] Pilot modules and workflows are agreed.
- [ ] Staff roles and access permissions are defined.
- [ ] Data import responsibilities and consent are agreed.
- [ ] Training dates are scheduled.
- [ ] Support channel and response expectations are documented.
- [ ] Pilot duration and review date are agreed.
- [ ] Success measures are documented.
- [ ] Paid continuation and pricing discussion are scheduled.

## 10. Outreach message

Use a personal, relevant message. Do not send the same generic pitch to every school.

<b>Initial message</b>

Hello \[Name\], I’m reaching out because I understand \[School Name\] is \[preparing to open / recently established / currently operating\] in \[Community, Ibadan\].

I’m building ACADIVA, a digital operating platform designed for Nigerian schools. It brings key school processes such as student records, administration, academic management and parent communication into one connected system.

I’d like to understand how your school currently handles these processes and see whether ACADIVA could be useful. We are speaking with a small number of schools in Ibadan for an initial pilot.

Would you be open to a short conversation or demonstration?

Regards,<br />ACADIVA

Personalise the opening with a real reason for contacting the school. Do not claim to have researched or verified a fact that has not been checked.

## 11. Partnerships and referral channels

Prioritise partnerships that provide trusted access to school decision-makers or reduce onboarding cost.

Potential partner categories:

- Private-school associations and local chapters.
- Education consultants and school establishment advisers.
- School suppliers and vendors.
- Teacher training and professional networks.
- Payment providers, once payment integration requirements are defined.
- Local implementation and training partners.

For each potential partner, document:

- What access or capability they can provide.
- What value ACADIVA can provide in return.
- Whether a formal agreement is required.
- Referral fees or commissions, if any, and who approves them.
- Data-sharing boundaries.
- Whether the relationship is exploratory, agreed or active.

Do not describe a potential partner as a confirmed partner until both sides have agreed.

## 12. First 30-day execution targets

These are initial activity targets for one founder-led acquisition effort, not forecasts or guaranteed outcomes.

| Activity | 30-day target |
| --- | --- |
| Nigerian schools discovered | 50 |
| Schools verified | 30 |
| Decision-makers identified | 20 |
| Personalised outreach attempts | 20 |
| Demonstrations completed | 5 |
| Pilot schools agreed | 2–3 |

The first 30 days should be used to test whether the target segments and channels produce real conversations. If the targets are missed, record why and adjust the approach rather than inflating the numbers.

### Weekly rhythm

- <b>Monday:</b> Build and qualify the lead list; plan visits and outreach.
- <b>Tuesday–Wednesday:</b> Contact schools, conduct visits and hold discovery conversations.
- <b>Thursday:</b> Run demonstrations and pilot discussions.
- <b>Friday:</b> Follow up, update the pipeline, review objections and report results.

Adapt the schedule to school calendars, opening hours and decision-maker availability.

## 13. Metrics that matter

### Acquisition

- Schools discovered and verified.
- Decision-makers reached.
- Qualified opportunities.
- Outreach response rate.
- Meetings and demos completed.
- Demo-to-pilot conversion.
- Pilot-to-paid conversion.
- Cost and time to acquire each active school.

### Activation and retention

- Time from agreement to first successful setup.
- Percentage of invited staff who activate accounts.
- Weekly active staff by role.
- Completion of agreed workflows.
- Parent account activation where included.
- Support requests and resolution time.
- Pilot completion and paid renewal.

### Product value

- Reduction in time spent on selected administrative tasks.
- Accuracy and completeness of records.
- Attendance and assessment completion.
- Fee records reconciled successfully.
- Parent communication reach and usefulness.
- School decision-maker satisfaction.

Do not use social impressions, raw registrations or demo attendance as substitutes for active school usage and paid retention.

## 14. Nigerian market and product requirements

The acquisition promise must match the product's actual readiness for Nigerian schools.

Prioritise validation of:

- Naira-denominated school billing and local payment expectations.
- Nigerian academic calendars, terms and session structures.
- School levels and local admissions practices.
- Configurable grading, continuous assessment and examination formats.
- WAEC, NECO and other relevant examination references where appropriate.
- Local parent communication habits and connectivity constraints.
- Reliable mobile use and low-bandwidth workflows.
- Data protection, school permissions, record security and export.
- Nigerian support hours, onboarding and training.

Do not assume every school follows identical calendars, grading rules or fee structures. Configuration should support legitimate differences.

## 15. Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Building ahead of customer evidence | Use interviews and pilots to validate demand before expanding scope |
| Schools express interest but do not adopt | Require agreed workflows, named coordinators and usage evidence |
| Free pilots do not convert | Set duration, scope and paid-continuation decision upfront |
| Product cannot support promised workflows | Demonstrate only tested capabilities; label roadmap items clearly |
| Founder becomes the only person who can operate the system | Measure staff independence and document repeatable onboarding |
| School data is inaccurate or imported without permission | Agree data ownership, authorisation, validation and import process |
| Support workload becomes unsustainable | Measure support time and define service boundaries |
| Expansion is too geographically broad | Prove repeatable acquisition and onboarding in Ibadan first |
| Partner relationship is overstated | Record partner status and obtain permission before public claims |

## 16. Decisions and open questions

### Current direction

- Nigeria is the only target market for this acquisition phase.
- Ibadan is the first market.
- Newly established and pre-opening private schools are the highest-priority segments to test.
- The first cohort target is 3–5 founding schools.
- The first 30-day activity targets are listed in Section 12.

### Still to validate

- Which school segment has the strongest urgency and willingness to pay.
- Which Ibadan communities should receive the first concentrated field effort.
- Which ACADIVA modules are genuinely ready for a pilot.
- Pilot duration and whether each pilot is free, subsidised or paid.
- Final onboarding and support boundaries.
- Pricing acceptance among proprietors of different school sizes.
- Which associations and referral partners are willing to make introductions.
- Whether the initial activity targets are realistic for founder capacity.

## 17. Operating principle

ACADIVA should earn expansion through evidence. First find Nigerian schools with a real operational problem, then help a small number use the product successfully, measure whether it improves their work and prove that they will pay to continue.

The immediate milestone is not nationwide coverage. It is a small, credible group of Nigerian schools that actively use ACADIVA and can demonstrate why it is worth keeping.

## 14. Prospective strategic partnership — AREAi / Project55 (20 November 2026)

### Partnership rationale

Explore whether ACADIVA could complement AREAi's education-access and digital-inclusion work by providing digital operating infrastructure to participating schools. The proposed fit is not to position ACADIVA as a replacement for AREAi's learning programmes or existing education technology partners. ACADIVA's potential contribution is school administration and operational infrastructure: student records, teacher workflows, attendance, academic records, results, parent communication and related school-management functions that are actually ready for deployment.

Project55 may provide a relevant wider digital-inclusion context, but no partnership, endorsement, funding, school access or procurement commitment from AREAi is assumed.

### Proposed pilot conversation

Explore a small, measurable pilot in approximately 3–5 schools, potentially beginning in Oyo State if AREAi and participating schools see a fit. The initial ACADIVA hardware concept is 10 shared Android tablets per school, with ACADIVA deployed and staff trained. A five-school pilot would therefore require up to 50 devices; at a preliminary target of ₦100,000 per device, the hardware-only estimate would be ₦5 million before deployment and support costs.

### Potential division of contribution — to negotiate

<b>ACADIVA could contribute:</b>

- School management software and configured school workspaces, limited to features that are built and tested.
- Shared tablets and device setup if funding is secured.
- School onboarding and teacher training.
- Technical support and usage reporting.
- Agreed measures of operational adoption and school experience.

<b>AREAi could potentially contribute, subject to its approval:</b>

- Advice on school selection and alignment with its education programmes.
- Introductions to eligible schools or programme stakeholders.
- Education-sector guidance and coordination.
- Consideration of sponsorship, co-funding or referral pathways for devices, if relevant to its programme and budget.

These are discussion points, not commitments or confirmed responsibilities.

### Pilot outcomes to measure

- Number of schools onboarded and still active.
- Number of devices deployed, operational and accounted for.
- Teachers trained and actively using ACADIVA.
- Attendance and academic workflows completed digitally.
- Time required for selected administrative tasks before and after deployment.
- Record completeness and error/correction rates.
- Parent access and engagement where relevant.
- School satisfaction, support burden and willingness to continue on a paid basis.

Any impact reporting must use aggregated or appropriately anonymised information. AREAi or any sponsor should not receive identifiable student records unless a lawful basis, appropriate safeguards and explicit authorised arrangements are in place.

### Suggested approach

Request an exploratory partnership discussion rather than immediately asking for funding. Present ACADIVA as a potential digital school-infrastructure partner and ask whether a jointly designed pilot would complement AREAi's priorities. Share a concise concept note with the problem, proposed schools, tablet model, responsibilities, safeguarding/data protections, budget assumptions, outcomes and sustainability plan.
